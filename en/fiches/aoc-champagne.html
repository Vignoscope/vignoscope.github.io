Grape varieties

Mainly Pinot Noir, Meunier and Chardonnay; Arbane, Petit Meslier, Pinot Blanc and Pinot Gris remain allowed.

Terroir

Upper Cretaceous chalk in the Marne (Montagne de Reims, Côte des Blancs, Marne valley), Kimmeridgian marl and limestone in the Côte des Bar (Aube).

A dual oceanic and continental influence, at the northern limit of vine growing: slow ripening and high acidity.

Typical style

Fine bubbles, freshness and finesse; the brioche and toast aromas come from ageing on the lees. Blanc de blancs (Chardonnay), blanc de noirs (Pinot Noir, Meunier), rosé by blending or by saignée, vintage or non-vintage.

Aromas often mentioned: apple, citrus, brioche, toast, red fruit (rosé).

Production rules

Traditional method: at least 15 months in the cellar for a non-vintage wine, 12 of them on the lees, and 3 years for a vintage. The dosage (liqueur d’expédition) determines the terms brut nature, extra-brut, brut, extra-dry, sec, demi-sec and doux.

Ageing

Non-vintage: 2 to 5 years after purchase; vintage and prestige cuvées: 10 to 30 years.

Food pairing

  • Aperitif
  • oysters and caviar
  • Chaource cheese
  • poultry in cream sauce (vintage)

Did you know?

Contrary to a widespread idea, Dom Pérignon did not “invent” champagne: this monk of the abbey of Hautvillers mainly perfected blending and pressing at the end of the 17th century.
