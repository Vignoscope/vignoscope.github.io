Practise: 6 questions drawn from the checked facts

Terroir

The leanest and finest gravel in the Médoc: white gravel, very little clay, on low mounds that roll towards the estuary. The poverty of the soil forces the vine to send its roots deep.

The estuary tempers spring frosts on the riverside mounds. Further west, towards Arsac, the soils are cooler and ripening is later.

Typical style

The most fragrant of the Médoc wines: violet, rose, blackcurrant and sweet spices, on fine, silky tannins. The power is very real, but dressed in lace.

Aromas often mentioned: violet, blackcurrant, raspberry, cedar, liquorice.

Production rules

Red wines only. A blend dominated by Cabernet Sauvignon, completed by Merlot, Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot. Whites produced in the area are released as AOC Bordeaux.

Ageing

10 to 30 years for the classified growths; 5 to 12 years for the others.

Food pairing

  • Roast rack of lamb with herbs
  • Beef fillet, rich jus
  • Pigeon with ceps

Did you know?

With 21 growths classified in 1855, from first to fifth rank, Margaux is the appellation most represented in the classification.
