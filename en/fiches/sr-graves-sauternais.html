Graves et Sauternais

South of Bordeaux, on the left bank of the Garonne: great reds and dry whites from the Graves, sweet wines from the Sauternais.

Open in the interactive atlas

The Graves owe their name to their soils of gravel and pebbles. To the north, on the edge of the city, Pessac-Léognan gathers the classified growths of Graves; further south, the Graves produce reds and dry whites, and the Graves Supérieures medium-sweet whites.

Where the Ciron meets the Garonne, the autumn mists encourage botrytis: Sauternes, Barsac and Cérons make sweet wines there.
