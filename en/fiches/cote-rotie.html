Practise: 6 questions drawn from the checked facts

Terroir

Dizzying slopes on the right bank, facing south-east, laid out in terraces (chaillées). To the north, the Côte Brune: dark mica-schist rich in iron oxide. To the south, the Côte Blonde: lighter gneiss and granite, lighter soils.

The orientation and slope capture maximum sunshine, hence the name “côte rôtie” (roasted slope); the river reflects the light and evens out temperature swings.

Typical style

Violet, blackberry, raspberry, pepper, smoked bacon, black olive; a silky, fragrant texture, more floral than Hermitage. The Côte Blonde gives finer wines, the Côte Brune more structured ones.

Aromas often mentioned: violet, blackberry, black pepper, smoked bacon, black olive.

Production rules

The specification covers only reds. Main grape variety: Syrah, at least 80% of the vines; Viognier, a white accessory grape, may be planted among them up to 20% of the vines, and the grapes are vinified together. The share of Viognier actually used varies by cuvée; it can add a floral perfume.

Ageing

8 to 25 years.

Food pairing

  • Leg of lamb with herbs
  • Pigeon with olives
  • Rigotte de Condrieu

Did you know?

According to legend, a lord of Ampuis left his two daughters, one dark-haired and the other blonde, the two slopes that now bear their names.
