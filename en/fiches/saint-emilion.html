Grape varieties

Mostly Merlot, Cabernet Franc, a little Cabernet Sauvignon and Malbec.

Terroir

Sand and gravel of the Dordogne plain, foot-slopes and clay-limestone around the medieval town.

Typical style

Supple, fruity reds, to drink younger than the Grands Crus.

Aromas often mentioned: plum, cherry, strawberry, sweet spices.

Production rules

Higher yields and shorter ageing than Saint-Émilion Grand Cru; no growth in this AOC can appear in the classification.

Ageing

3 to 8 years.

Food pairing

  • Lamprey bordelaise
  • entrecôte grilled over vine cuttings
  • Saint-Émilion macarons

Did you know?

The jurisdiction of Saint-Émilion, which corresponds to the appellation area, was the first wine landscape inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list, in 1999.

Communes of the area (9)

Libourne, Saint-Christophe-des-Bardes, Saint-Hippolyte, Saint-Laurent-des-Combes, Saint-Pey-d'Armens, Saint-Sulpice-de-Faleyrens, Saint-Émilion, Saint-Étienne-de-Lisse, Vignonet.
