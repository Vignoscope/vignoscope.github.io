Wine region

Champagne

Chalk, blending and prise de mousse: the most famous sparkling wine in the world.

Open in the interactive atlas

In brief

Champagne is a sparkling wine whose bubbles come from a second fermentation in the bottle. A non-vintage champagne ages for at least 15 months before release, a vintage for at least 36 months.

  • A strictly delimited geographical area.
  • Seven grape varieties allowed (including Pinot Noir, Meunier and Chardonnay), plus one variety of interest for adaptation, Voltis.

Checked on 5 November 2026 (method, minimum times, number of grape varieties). Sources: Comité Champagne, fiche « AOC Champagne ».

Area
≈ 34 000 ha
UNESCO
Hillsides, houses and cellars (2015)

A dual oceanic and continental influence, at the northern limit of the vine: low average temperatures, spring frosts and high-acid grapes, ideal for sparkling wine.

Geology and soils

In the Marne, a chalk subsoil that stores water and returns it to the vine; dug out, it houses kilometres of cellars at a constant temperature. In the Aube, the Côte des Bar lies on Kimmeridgian marl and limestone, close to those of Chablis.

Taste profile

Fine bubbles, freshness, notes of citrus, apple, brioche and toast from ageing on the lees. Blanc de blancs, blanc de noirs, rosés, vintages; the still wines are released as Coteaux Champenois and Rosé des Riceys.

Appellations

  • Champagne
  • Coteaux champenois
  • Rosé des Riceys
