Grape varieties

Riesling, Gewurztraminer, Pinot Gris, Muscat, Sylvaner, Pinot Blanc (often blended with Auxerrois), Chasselas; Pinot Noir for reds and rosés.

Terroir

A mosaic resulting from the subsidence of the Rhine rift: granite, gneiss, schist, sandstone, limestone, marl, volcanic soils.

Typical style

Aromatic whites, often dry, sometimes with residual sugar; “Edelzwicker” and “Gentil” for blends.

Aromas often mentioned: citrus, peach, lychee (Gewurztraminer), smoke (Pinot Gris), flowers.

Production rules

Thirteen denominations may be added to the name Alsace (Bergheim, Côtes de Barr, Klevener de Heiligenstein, Vallée Noble, Wolxheim…). The terms vendanges tardives and sélection de grains nobles apply to wines from overripe or botrytised grapes. Bottling is compulsory within the region, in the Alsace flute.

Ageing

2 to 10 years depending on the grape variety.

Did you know?

Klevener de Heiligenstein is made from Savagnin Rose, an old grape variety related to Gewurztraminer, grown around the village of Heiligenstein.
