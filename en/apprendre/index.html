Learn the fundamentals of wine

A free course in four levels, from first steps to the main regions: short lessons to read a label, understand sugar and tannins, tell Bordeaux from Burgundy or champagne from crémant, then guides and paths to go further.

Start in the interactive atlas

The course: French wine, level by level

4 levels, 11 lessons of 5 to 7 minutes (about 64 minutes in all). Each lesson has a goal, an activity, corrected questions and a summary, with its sources. In the interactive atlas, a placement test tells you where to start.

Level 1 · First steps

Tell a grape variety from an appellation, read a label, understand the sugar terms, describe a wine on the palate and place the main regions.

  1. Grape variety, appellation, terroir · 5 min
    By the end, you will be able to tell a grape variety name from an appellation name on a label, and say what the word terroir covers.
  2. Reading a French wine label · 6 min
    By the end, you will be able to find the appellation, the vintage and the bottler on a label, and tell a mandatory label term from an optional one.
  3. Dry, medium-dry, sweet: sugar on the label · 5 min
    By the end, you will know what sec, demi-sec, moelleux and doux mean on a still wine, and why a “sec” sparkling wine is sweeter than a “brut”.
  4. Acidity, tannins, alcohol: describing a wine on the palate · 5 min
    By the end, you will be able to recognise acidity, tannins and alcohol on the palate, and explain why a young red can taste harsh.
  5. Finding your way around the main regions · 6 min
    By the end, you will be able to place four major wine regions on the map and link each one to a landmark: a river or a climate.

Level 2 · The main regions

Recognise what makes Bordeaux, Burgundy, the Rhône and Champagne: grape varieties, geographical landmarks, ways of naming the wines.

  1. Bordeaux and Burgundy: blending or naming the place · 6 min
    By the end, you will be able to say which grape varieties make the wines of Bordeaux and Burgundy, tell the Left Bank from the Right Bank, and read a Burgundy village name.
  2. Northern and southern Rhône · 6 min
    By the end, you will be able to say whether a Rhône wine is more likely from the north or the south, from its grape variety, its terrain and its climate.
  3. Champagne and crémant: the sparkling wines of France · 5 min
    By the end, you will be able to say what champagne and crémants have in common and what sets them apart: the area, the grape varieties and the minimum ageing times.

Coming soon: Loire Valley, from Muscadet to Sancerre · Alsace, the grape on the label · The South, Languedoc-Roussillon and Provence.

Level 3 · Comparing and going further

Compare two appellations from their rules, reason through real situations and read a specification.

  1. Comparing Vinsobres and Gigondas · 6 min
    By the end, you will be able to name one thing Vinsobres and Gigondas share and two differences, and say which colours each can produce.
  2. Review: real-life situations · 7 min
    By the end, you will have used the notions of the five lessons in concrete situations and will know which ones to strengthen.
  3. Going further: vines or bottle? · 7 min · advanced
    By the end, you will be able to tell a rule about an estate’s vines (the planting mix, or encépagement) from a rule about the wine itself (the blend, the colour), and draw the right conclusions.
