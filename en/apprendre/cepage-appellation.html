Grape variety, appellation, terroir

Do the interactive lesson (activity and questions)

About 5 minutes · beginner

Goal. By the end, you will be able to tell a grape variety name from an appellation name on a label, and say what the word terroir covers.

Key points

  1. A grape variety (cépage) is a variety of vine: Syrah, Chardonnay, Sauvignon, Merlot… The same variety can be grown in many regions of the world.
  2. An appellation (AOC, or PDO under EU law) is a protected place name: Chablis, Sancerre, Gigondas… Its specification sets the production area, the permitted grape varieties and the rules for growing and making the wine.
  3. Terroir brings together a setting (soil, relief, climate, landscape) and the shared know-how of the people who work it. It is not just the soil.

Example: Chablis and Alsace, two ways of naming a wine

On a Chablis label, the word “Chardonnay” is often missing: the appellation’s specification allows only this variety, so the place name is enough to tell you.

In Alsace, by contrast, the custom is to name the grape variety (Riesling, Gewurztraminer, Pinot Gris…) next to the Alsace appellation.

See the answers
  • Syrah: Grape variety. A vine variety grown from the Rhône Valley to Australia.
  • Sancerre: Appellation. An appellation of the Centre-Loire.
  • Chardonnay: Grape variety. The white grape of Chablis and the Côte de Beaune.
  • Gigondas: Appellation. A cru of the southern Rhône, at the foot of the Dentelles de Montmirail.
  • Merlot: Grape variety. The most planted grape variety in Bordeaux.
  • Pauillac: Appellation. A village appellation of the Médoc.

Question 1 of 3

On a label reading “Sancerre · Domaine des Exemples · Sauvignon”, which word is the appellation?

  1. Sancerre
  2. Sauvignon
  3. Domaine des Exemples
  4. None of the three
See the answer

A. Sancerre

Sancerre is the protected place name: the appellation. Sauvignon is the grape variety, and “Domaine des Exemples” a fictitious producer.

Question 2 of 3

Terroir is…

  1. The administrative region of the vineyard
  2. The type of soil, and nothing else
  3. A setting (soil, relief, climate) and the know-how of the people who work it
  4. The main grape variety of a region
See the answer

C. A setting (soil, relief, climate) and the know-how of the people who work it

For the International Organisation of Vine and Wine (OIV, 2010), terroir combines an identifiable physical and biological environment with the practices of winegrowers, which give wines distinctive characteristics.

Question 3 of 3

Why can a Chablis label do without the word “Chardonnay”?

  1. Because the Chablis appellation allows only Chardonnay
  2. Because Chablis blends several grape varieties
  3. Because the law forbids naming a grape variety
  4. Because the grape variety always appears on the back label
See the answer

A. Because the Chablis appellation allows only Chardonnay

The Chablis specification allows only Chardonnay: the name of the appellation is enough to tell you the grape variety.

Remember

  • Grape variety = a variety of vine; appellation = a protected place name, with its rules.
  • In France, the label puts the appellation first; the grape variety is optional, except where custom says otherwise (Alsace).
  • Terroir = a natural setting and the know-how of the people who farm it.

Vocabulary

Grape variety (cépage)
A variety of vine (Vitis vinifera): Merlot, Pinot Noir, Syrah, Chardonnay… Each variety brings its own aromas, structure and ripening rhythm.
AOC / PDO
Appellation d’origine contrôlée (protected designation of origin at EU level). It ties a wine to a defined area and to a specification: grape varieties, yields, growing and winemaking practices.
Terroir
The interplay of soil, subsoil, climate, aspect and human know-how that gives a wine a recognisable identity.
