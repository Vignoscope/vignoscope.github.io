Bordeaux and Burgundy: blending or naming the place

Do the interactive lesson (activity and questions)

About 6 minutes · beginner · do first: Finding your way around the main regions

Goal. By the end, you will be able to say which grape varieties make the wines of Bordeaux and Burgundy, tell the Left Bank from the Right Bank, and read a Burgundy village name.

Key points

  1. Bordeaux most often blends several grape varieties: Merlot, Cabernet Franc and Cabernet Sauvignon for reds; Sémillon and Sauvignon for whites.
  2. Three waterways shape Bordeaux: the Garonne, the Dordogne and the Gironde estuary. On the Left Bank (Médoc, Graves), gravel soils favour Cabernet Sauvignon; on the Right Bank (Libournais), clay suits Merlot.
  3. Burgundy makes almost everything from two grape varieties, Pinot Noir and Chardonnay, and tells its wines apart by place: regional appellations, villages, premiers crus, grands crus, and more than 600 climats.

Example: two labels

“Pauillac”: a Left Bank red, most often a blend dominated by Cabernet Sauvignon.

“Gevrey-Chambertin”: a red from the Côte de Nuits, made from Pinot Noir; the village name is the name of the appellation.

  2. Merlot

B. Merlot

Clay suits Merlot, which is widely planted on the Right Bank; the gravels of the Left Bank favour Cabernet Sauvignon. This is a tendency, not a rule.

A. The village, which is also the appellation

Gevrey-Chambertin is a village in the Côte de Nuits; its name is the name of the appellation. The red wine there is made from Pinot Noir.

Remember

  • Bordeaux blends: Merlot, Cabernet Franc, Cabernet Sauvignon for reds; Sémillon and Sauvignon for whites.
  • Left Bank (Médoc, Graves): gravel and Cabernet Sauvignon; Right Bank (Libournais): clay and Merlot. A tendency, not a rule.
  • Burgundy: Pinot Noir and Chardonnay, and above all the place, from regional appellations to grands crus.

Vocabulary

Blending (assemblage)
Mixing wines from different grape varieties, plots or vats to build a more complete wine. A pillar of Bordeaux and the southern Rhône.
Graves
Soils of gravel and pebbles laid down by the ancient Garonne. Warm and free-draining, they suit Cabernet Sauvignon; they also gave their name to the region south of Bordeaux.
Climat (Burgundy)
A precisely bounded vineyard plot, named for centuries, whose wine expresses a character of its own. The 1,247 climats of Burgundy have been on the UNESCO World Heritage List since 2015.
