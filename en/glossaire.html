Wine glossary

75 French wine terms defined in one or two sentences, with the French word as the headword. In the atlas, these words are underlined in the texts and explained with a click.

ABCDEFGHIKLMNOPRSTV
Agriculture biologique (organic farming)
No synthetic herbicides, insecticides or fertilisers; copper and sulphur remain allowed. EU certification (green leaf logo).
AOC / PDO
Appellation d’origine contrôlée (protected designation of origin at EU level). It ties a wine to a defined area and to a specification: grape varieties, yields, growing and winemaking practices.
Argilo-calcaire (clay-limestone)
A soil mixing clay (water reserve, coolness) and limestone (finesse, tension). Typical of Bordeaux’s Right Bank and of many Burgundy slopes.
Arzelle
In Condrieu, a sandy topsoil formed from decomposed granite and mica. Light and poor, it suits Viognier.
Blending (assemblage)
Mixing wines from different grape varieties, plots or vats to build a more complete wine. A pillar of Bordeaux and the southern Rhône.
Barrique
The 225-litre Bordeaux barrel, usually of French oak. New, it brings vanilla and toasty notes and tannins; used, it lets the wine breathe without marking it.
Bâtonnage (lees stirring)
Stirring the fine lees back into suspension in the barrel with a stick. It gives whites richness and roundness, in Burgundy especially.
Biodynamie (biodynamics)
Organic viticulture supplemented by preparations (horn manure, silica, herbal teas) and a calendar inspired by lunar rhythms. Certifications: Demeter, Biodyvin.
Blanc de Blancs
A white wine made only from white grapes; in Champagne, most often from Chardonnay. A Blanc de Noirs is a white made from black grapes.
Botrytis cinerea (noble rot)
A fungus that, with alternating morning mists and sunny afternoons, pierces the grape skin and dries the berry out. The juice concentrates in sugar, acidity and aromas: it is the basis of Sauternes.
Cabernet Franc
A red grape, parent of Cabernet Sauvignon. Aromas of raspberry, violet and graphite; the star of Cheval Blanc and of the Loire Valley (Chinon).
Carafage (decanting)
Pouring into a carafe to aerate a young, closed wine, or to separate an old wine from its sediment (this is called décantation, to be done gently).
Caudalie
A unit of aromatic persistence: one caudalie is one second during which the aromas remain noticeable after swallowing or spitting the wine.
Grape variety (cépage)
A variety of vine (Vitis vinifera): Merlot, Pinot Noir, Syrah, Chardonnay… Each variety brings its own aromas, structure and ripening rhythm.
Chaillées
Narrow terraces held up by dry-stone walls on the steep slopes of the northern Rhône (Côte-Rôtie, Condrieu, Saint-Joseph).
Classement de 1855 (1855 classification)
The ranking of Gironde wines drawn up for the Paris Universal Exhibition: 61 reds (60 from the Médoc plus Haut-Brion) divided into five growths, and the sweet wines of Sauternes and Barsac. Only major change: Mouton Rothschild promoted to first growth in 1973.
Climat (Burgundy)
A precisely bounded vineyard plot, named for centuries, whose wine expresses a character of its own. The 1,247 climats of Burgundy have been on the UNESCO World Heritage List since 2015.
Clos
A vineyard enclosed by a wall, a legacy of the medieval monasteries in Burgundy (Clos de Vougeot, Clos de Bèze).
Côte / coteau (slope)
A hillside planted with vines. The aspect (often east to south) and the slope improve sunshine and drainage.
Cru
A place or estate recognised for the quality of its wine. The meaning varies by region: a château in Bordeaux, a climat in Burgundy, a commune in the Rhône or Beaujolais.
Demi-muid
A 500 to 600-litre barrel. Its lower wood-to-wine ratio limits the oaky taste: widely used in the Rhône.
Dosage
The addition, after a sparkling wine is disgorged, of a liqueur d’expédition (wine and sugar). It sets the label term: brut nature, extra brut, brut, extra dry, sec, demi-sec or doux.
Égrappage (destemming)
Separating the berries from the stems before maceration, to avoid green or vegetal tannins.
Élevage (maturation)
The period between the end of fermentation and bottling. The wine matures in tank, barrel or amphora; how long and in what shape its texture and aromas.
Fermentation malolactique (malolactic fermentation)
A second fermentation, carried out by bacteria, that turns malic acid (sharp, “green apple”) into softer lactic acid. It softens the wine and can bring buttery notes.
Feuillette
A small Chablis barrel of about 132 litres (half a pièce), still used by a few traditional estates.
Foudre
A very large wooden vessel (from 10 to more than 100 hectolitres). It allows slow micro-oxygenation without marking the wine.
Galets roulés (rounded pebbles)
Large quartzite stones polished by the Rhône and by glaciers. They drain the soil, store heat by day and release it at night, helping the grapes ripen fully.
Garrigue
Mediterranean scrub on limestone ground (thyme, rosemary, rockrose, kermes oak). By extension, the dried-herb aroma typical of southern French wines.
Grand Cru
The top of the Burgundy hierarchy: 33 exceptional climats, about 1% of production. The name of the climat is enough on the label (e.g. “Chambertin”).
Graves
Soils of gravel and pebbles laid down by the ancient Garonne. Warm and free-draining, they suit Cabernet Sauvignon; they also gave their name to the region south of Bordeaux.
HVE
Haute valeur environnementale (high environmental value): a French certification of the estate (biodiversity, plant protection, fertilisation, irrigation). Less demanding than organic.
IGP
Indication géographique protégée (protected geographical indication, PGI). A larger area and more flexible rules than a PDO; the grape variety may appear on the label (e.g. IGP Pays d’Oc).
Kimméridgien (Kimmeridgian)
A geological stage of the Upper Jurassic (≈ 155 million years ago). In Chablis: alternating marl and limestone packed with small fossil oysters (Exogyra virgula), linked to the minerality of the wines.
Larmes (tears, legs)
Drops that run down the side of the glass after swirling. They mainly signal richness in alcohol and sugar, not quality.
Lauzes
In Tavel, flat chips of limestone that cover some soils; they reflect heat and protect the soil from evaporation.
Macération (maceration)
Contact between the juice and the skins that extracts colour, tannins and aromas. Short for a rosé, long for a great red for ageing.
Macération carbonique (carbonic maceration)
Fermentation of whole bunches under carbon dioxide, which starts inside the berries. It gives fruity, supple wines with notes of banana or pear drops (Beaujolais).
Marne (marl)
A sedimentary rock mixing clay and limestone, often rich in fossils. It holds water and gives broad wines.
Méthode ancestrale
Sparkle obtained from a single fermentation, finished in the bottle without adding sugar or yeast. The wine keeps some of the grape’s sugar (Clairette de Die “méthode dioise ancestrale”, Blanquette méthode ancestrale, Bugey Cerdon, Gaillac).
Méthode traditionnelle (traditional method)
Sparkle from a second fermentation in the bottle, followed by ageing on the lees, riddling and disgorging. The method of Champagne and the crémants.
Vintage (millésime)
The harvest year. The weather of the year (frost, heat, rain) gives the wine a character of its own.
Mistral
A violent north wind that sweeps down the Rhône Valley. It keeps the vines healthy (less disease), concentrates the grapes and clears the sky.
Molasse
A deposit of soft sandstone and clay built up at the foot of hills. It is found in the Fronsadais, around Bergerac and in the southern Rhône.
Monocépage (single-variety wine)
A wine made from a single grape variety, such as Pinot Noir in red Burgundy or Syrah in Cornas.
Monopole
A climat (or lieu-dit) wholly owned by a single estate, such as La Romanée-Conti or the Clos des Ducs.
Négociant
A house that buys grapes, musts or wines to mature, blend and sell them under its own name. Some also own large vineyards.
Ouillage (topping up)
Regularly filling up barrels to make up for evaporation and keep the wine out of contact with air. Vin jaune, by contrast, is never topped up: a veil of yeast forms on its surface.
Passerillage
Concentrating sugars by drying the grape, on the vine (Jurançon, Pacherenc) or on racks (vin de paille), without botrytis.
Phylloxéra (phylloxera)
An aphid from America that ravaged the French vineyard from the 1860s. The remedy: grafting European vines onto resistant American rootstocks.
Pièce
The Burgundy barrel of about 228 litres, the equivalent of the Bordeaux barrique.
Potentiel de garde (ageing potential)
How long a wine gains complexity in the cellar before declining. It depends on acidity, tannins, sugar and overall balance.
Premier Cru
In Burgundy, a high-quality climat ranked below the Grands Crus. It is shown with the name of the commune: “Volnay 1er Cru Clos des Ducs”.
Rancio
The taste of oxidative development (walnut, cocoa, coffee, spice) in vins doux naturels aged for a long time in contact with air, in barrels or in glass demijohns left in the sun.
Récoltant-manipulant
In Champagne, a grower who makes and sells champagne from his own grapes (“RM” on the label), as opposed to négociant houses (“NM”).
Rendement (yield)
The quantity of wine produced per hectare, in hectolitres (hl/ha). Each AOC sets a maximum; low yields often concentrate the grapes.
Rimage
A vintage Banyuls or Maury, aged briefly away from air and bottled young to keep the colour and fruit of the grape.
Robe (colour, appearance)
The look of the wine: colour, intensity, tints, clarity. The rim (the edge of the tilted wine) tells its age: purple when young, brick or amber with time.
Safres
Compacted marine sands (soft sandstone) of the southern Rhône. They give finer, more fragrant wines than the pebbles.
Saignée
A rosé technique: part of the juice is drawn off a tank of red after a short maceration. In Tavel, one speaks rather of skin maceration of 12 to 48 hours.
Schist
A layered rock formed from clays transformed by pressure. Poor and free-draining, it gives back heat: Banyuls, Collioure, Faugères, Saint-Chinian, the “black Anjou”, Côte-Rôtie (mica schists).
Second vin (second wine)
A wine from young vines or from lots not chosen for a château’s grand vin. More affordable and ready to drink sooner.
Solera
An ageing system from Jerez: each year, part of the wine is drawn from a perpetual reserve, which is then topped up with wine from the latest harvest.
Sur lie
A term reserved for wines left on their fine lees all winter, without racking, until bottling in spring (Muscadet, Gros Plant). It brings richness, freshness and sometimes a slight prickle.
Tanins (tannins)
Compounds from the skins, seeds, stems and wood. They give the feeling of astringency and the backbone of red wines, and soften with time.
Terroir
The interplay of soil, subsoil, climate, aspect and human know-how that gives a wine a recognisable identity.
Tries successives (successive pickings)
Harvesting in several passes through the same vineyard to pick only the berries at the right stage (e.g. noble rot). A slow, costly practice.
Tuffeau
A yellow or white micaceous chalk from the Turonian, typical of Touraine and the Saumur area. Easy to dig, it houses cave cellars and gives fine, taut wines.
Vendange entière (whole-bunch fermentation)
Making wine from grapes with their stems (bunches not destemmed). It can bring freshness, spicy or floral notes and a firmer tannic frame.
Vendanges tardives (VT, late harvest)
A term reserved, notably in Alsace and Jurançon, for wines from overripe grapes, with a regulated minimum sugar level. In Alsace, the term Sélection de grains nobles (SGN) also requires berries affected by noble rot.
Vente en primeur (en primeur sales)
The Bordeaux system: wines are sold in the spring after the harvest, still in barrel, and delivered about two years later. The brokers and négociants of “la Place de Bordeaux” organise distribution.
Vin de France
A category without a geographical indication. Great freedom of blends and grape varieties; some winegrowers deliberately place unconventional cuvées there.
Vin de liqueur
Grape must fortified with a spirit before any fermentation, then aged (Pineau des Charentes, Floc de Gascogne, Macvin du Jura). Not to be confused with vin doux naturel, fortified during fermentation.
Vin doux naturel
A fortified wine: neutral alcohol is added during fermentation to stop it and keep the grape’s sugar (Banyuls, Rasteau, Muscat de Beaumes-de-Venise).
Vin jaune
A Jura speciality: Savagnin aged for at least six years and three months under a veil of yeast, without topping up. Notes of walnut and curry; 62 cl clavelin bottle.
